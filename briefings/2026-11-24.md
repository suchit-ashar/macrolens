## Global Macro
**Rates and liquidity.** The US 10-year and 2-year Treasury par yields were 4.92% and 4.63% on 23 November; the 2s10s spread was 29 basis points. The curve is positively sloped, but the level of real yields remains restrictive: the 10-year inflation-indexed Treasury yield was 2.77% on 20 November. SOFR was 3.92% and the effective federal funds rate 3.88% on 20 November, the latest published overnight reference-rate observations available at this cut-off. The Fed's target range is 3.75%–4.00%, held on 28 October after a September increase. [Fed H.15, 23 Nov release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/); [New York Fed SOFR](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/reference-rates/sofr); [FRED SOFR, 20 Nov observation](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SOFR).

Learning: SOFR measures secured overnight borrowing against Treasury collateral; it is a key reference for floating-rate funding. Treasury yields are market prices for government borrowing across maturities, not the Fed's policy rate.

**Inflation and employment.** September PCE inflation was 3.6% year over year, with core at 3.1%; monthly PCE and core PCE rose 0.5% and 0.3%. October CPI rose 0.4% monthly and core CPI 0.3%, but the missing October 2025 comparison prevents a valid October year-over-year CPI rate. October payrolls increased by 165,000 and unemployment was 4.1%; average hourly earnings rose 3.1% year over year. [BEA, 29 Oct](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-september-2026); [BLS, 10 Nov](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/CPI.htm); [October jobs report, 6 Nov](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.htm).

**Fed expectations and dollar.** The October 27–28 FOMC minutes showed that nearly all participants saw a December increase as likely if inflation persisted; two members dissented in favour of a hike at the October meeting. Reuters reported on 24 November that futures priced a 95% probability of a 25-basis-point December increase and 4.9% for a 50-basis-point move. DXY was around 104.32 in the latest Reuters snapshot, near a 20-month high. A stronger dollar and higher US real yields can tighten global financial conditions, pressure emerging-market currencies and raise the hurdle rate for long-duration equities. [FOMC minutes, published Nov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20261028.htm); [Reuters, 24 Nov](https://www.reuters.com/business/dollar-climbs-renewed-ai-euphoria-fuels-fed-hike-bets-2026-11-24/).

Inference: The near-term risk is not simply another 25-basis-point move; it is the possibility that markets have to price a longer sequence of restrictive policy if services inflation and wages stay firm.

## India ↔ Global Linkages
- US real yields and a stronger DXY → tighter external financial conditions → potential pressure on emerging-market currencies and foreign portfolio risk budgets.
- Brent lower → lower prospective Indian import and input costs → possible support for the current account, inflation and downstream margins; the rupee and local price pass-through determine the realised benefit.
- AI-led US technology rebound → stronger global semiconductor sentiment → support for Indian IT sentiment, but not necessarily a direct earnings uplift for Indian IT services.
- Higher US rate expectations → higher global discount rates → valuation pressure on long-duration equities, even when near-term earnings news is positive.
